Four Gates, Four Artifacts

Pre-registered measurements on the walnut PCCT dataset. Every apparent signal so far has dissolved into an instrumentation artifact under control. This documents what was measured, what it means, and what stopped.

Walnut PCCT · Zhou et al., Sci Data 12, 1955 (2025) CPU only · no GPU Measurement phase — no method development
Kill testKILL Σ diagonal to 0.010
Test 0MOSTLY ARTIFACT 96–98% tube drift
Gate AFAIL below systematic floor
Gate BMARGINAL 17.8% double-covered
Overlap × gradient NOT ESTABLISHED 2 samples, position-locked

The bottom line

The planned method — a self-supervised decomposition loss trained on the known cross-channel noise covariance — requires residual dependency between the Low and High energy bins after the basis change from (Total, High). Three independent measurements say that dependency is not resolvable in this data. The method reduces to a change of variables.

Separately, the claim that overlapping bed positions disagree more at sharp edges is visible in the data, but its signature tracks reconstruction geometry rather than detector physics, and it does not survive matching the cone angle.

What the gates bought

Four times, the naive reading of a result looked like a green light, and four times it was an artifact — twice tube drift, once a bug in the simulator that faked precisely the effect being hunted, once cone-beam geometry. Cost to find out: about a day. Cost of discovering it after building the network: months, with a real chance of never knowing the result was spurious.

01

Kill test — is the noise covariance non-trivial?

Claim under test

Spatial–spectral noise covariance in the detector is large enough to be worth modelling through FDK and the 2×2 material-decomposition inverse.

1440 repeat views of a static water phantom, eight ROIs, harmonic detrending of the ±27.5-channel off-axis wobble on object ROIs only.

Peak |correlation| is 0.0100 overall and 0.0078 in air — five times below the KILL threshold. Fano sits at 1.00–1.02, i.e. pure Poisson. The x and y lag profiles are symmetric, so no residual wobble leaked in, and var_removed falls from 89.4% at the shadow edge to 1.3% at deep_centre, ruling out over-fitted detrending.

Unresolved — blocks interpretation

At 100 µm pitch, Mode1x1, 15 keV threshold and 80 kVp, a diagonal Σ is physically surprising: published PCD nearest-neighbour correlations at this pitch run 0.05–0.3. Either the detector performs on-chip charge summing (a real and useful finding) or these projections are not as raw as assumed (a broken measurement). Until that is settled from the vendor or the source paper, the null is uninterpretable.

02

Test 0 — what survives the incumbent calibration?

Claim under test

After a per-pixel polynomial calibration, systematic structure remains in the residuals — low-rank enough that a shared model could exploit it.

56 PMMA×Al slab combinations (7×8, including zero-thickness rows), full detector, 1,015,881 good pixels (97.51%).

Confound 1 — per-acquisition drift

96–98% of the residual variance is a single global offset per combination — one flat number per acquisition, i.e. tube-output drift between 56 separate step-and-shoot exposures. It is spatially flat, essentially random in thickness, and 0.99-correlated between bins. Run the pre-registered rule on raw residuals and it fires ALIVE on that scalar.

Once the offset is removed and the residual whitened, amplitude is modest — 1.37–1.51× the noise floor at degree 3. But what remains is strikingly low-rank: top-5 energy 0.956 against an iid null of 0.180, effective rank 1.49 against a null rank of 28. The spatial pattern is detector-module structure — blocks aligned to the 129-channel tile period and a sharp discontinuity at detector row 252, which the authors' own code independently confirms by interpolating slices 252–254 as a physical gap.

Two further traps found here: air_table_*.raw is byte-identical to PMMA_0_AL_0, making that combination self-normalising (P ≡ 0, zero noise) and requiring its exclusion; and the brief's cross-bin diagnostic used Total vs High, which is trivially correlated at √(H/T) ≈ 0.84 because High is a subset of Total. Low vs High is the informative pair.

03

Reconstruction pipeline — enabling work

The authors' code is MATLAB + TIGRE, Windows-only, CUDA ≥8 GB, 64 GB RAM. None of that is available here, and their repository contains no reconstruction algorithm — ProjDataRecon.m builds a geometry struct and calls TIGRE's GPU FDK. The pipeline was therefore ported to Python and the FDK written from scratch.

STEPC (recovered from source, previously undocumented): err = c1·P_low + c2·P_high + c3·P_low² + c4·P_high² + c5·P_low·P_high P_corrected = P_bin − err
  • FDK validated to 0.2% — three analytic spheres recover their true µ at 0.998 / 1.000 / 0.998, correct positions, zero background. This also settled the angle convention: the opposite sign silently drops off-axis structure.
  • Air reconstructs at −1001 ± 49 HU against a true −1000 — an end-to-end check of FDK scaling, air normalisation, STEPC and the authors' HU table simultaneously.
  • Four bed positions stitched into a continuous 60 mm volume with no visible discontinuity at slab boundaries.
  • A CUDA/TIGRE port is also written and its geometry verified numerically, but is untested — there is no GPU on this machine.
Two metadata corrections

InpRot is in degrees, not radians — their code calls deg2rad, so detector roll is 0.12°, not 7°. And Python TIGRE reverses MATLAB TIGRE's axis order for nDetector, nVoxel and the projection array; getting it wrong still runs, it just returns a scrambled volume.

04

Gate A — is there residual Low/High dependency?

Claim under test

Cov(Low, High) is non-zero. If it is zero, the planned loss carries no information beyond a change of basis.

A1–A2 — calibrating the instrument

The prior rho_LH ≈ +0.002 was uninterpretable because the simulator produced near-zero cross-bin covariance by construction. Two things had to be fixed before a recovery curve meant anything.

Confound 2 — drift leak in the simulator

The generator computed the beam-hardening split fraction from a drift-modulated intensity, making frac_H fluctuate view-to-view — accidentally the same mechanism needed to create genuine cross-bin covariance. Predicted spurious Cov(L,H) = −54.5 in air; observed −50.7. It put the null at −0.0353, identical to the predicted signal at the smallest injection. The gate would have passed on an artifact and been blind to p = 0.01.

The spec warns that a symmetric two-way swap cancels. In fact every fixed-probability spill cancels, one-way included: binomial thinning of a Poisson yields independent parts. What survives is a fluctuating split fraction, giving Cov(L,H) = −λ²·p².

Formally these are 5.9σ, 1.1σ and 3.3σ from zero. Three things overturn that reading:

  • Two physically equivalent air ROIs disagree by 3.4σ (0.00159 ± 0.00047). The real systematic floor is ≈0.0016 — larger than any measured value. Against that floor all three are consistent with zero.
  • The mandated detrend moves deep_centre by 23σ (+0.0056 → +0.0007) while removing only 1.3% of variance. The answer is set by a preprocessing step that explains almost nothing.
  • Every value is 18–90× below the smallest injection A2 could resolve, and the sign is positive where the calibrated physical mechanism gives negative. Positive cross-bin covariance is the signature of residual flux fluctuation, not spectral overlap.

Verdict: FAIL. Strictly this sits between the spec's Fail and Ambiguous rows — but the A/B difference required for Pass is clearly unmet, since population A's internal scatter exceeds the A–B gap. Both branches prescribe the same action: stop.

05

Gate B — is there enough paired signal?

Reconstructing as the authors do (sVoxel(3) = 15 mm, exactly the couch step) truncates every slab to abutting blocks and yields 0% overlap by construction. That is a reconstruction choice, not a property of the acquisition. Physically available overlap is set by the detector's axial FOV at isocentre against the couch step:

axial FOV = 505 px × 0.1 mm / (SDD/SID) = 50.5 / 2.3127 = 21.84 mm couch step = 15.00 mm → ~6.8 mm genuinely shared at the rotation axis

Invalid check — my error

The registration step reported +0.59, +1.56 and +0.00 mm against a nominal +15.00 mm. That is not a registration failure but a flawed check: it cross-correlates each slab's mean z-profile, which is dominated by the cone-beam sensitivity envelope common to every position, so it locks onto the envelope rather than the anatomy. The 15 mm spacing is independently solid from objViewCouchPosition and from anatomical continuity in the stitched volume, but this particular number measures nothing.

06

Overlap × gradient — can redundancy see object-dependent error?

Claim under test

Overlapping bed positions should disagree, and disagree more at sharp edges than in flat regions. An upward trend would be direct observation of object-dependent detector error.

Design: each bed position is reconstructed as two independent half-scans, so a matched control shares the geometry, cone angle and streak structure but cannot contain cross-position disagreement. The statistic is exact under noise for any heterogeneous σ:

R = sqrt( <DC²> / ((<DW1²> + <DW2²>)/2) ) R = 1 exactly under pure noise

Replication — and what it rules out

Walnut 2 reproduces walnut 1 pair-for-pair at matched cone angle: 1.073 vs 1.074, 0.955 vs 0.936, 1.182 vs 1.135. Every curvature is positive in both samples, and pair 2→3 is the near-null in both. So the pair-to-pair pattern is structural, not object-dependent — it tracks position in the scan rather than the specimen. That cuts against object-dependent detector error, which should follow the object; two different walnuts should not produce the same ordering.

The raw trend is real and monotone in all three pairs. But it does not survive control:

  • R is U-shaped in cone-angle asymmetry, minimised exactly where the two positions view a voxel at equal cone-angle magnitude and roughly doubling toward the band edges. All three curvatures are positive. That is the signature of FDK cone-beam error, which has no reason to care about a voxel's height in the slab — but detector physics has no reason to produce it either.
  • Extrapolating to matched cone angle collapses the effect from 1.39–1.40 to 0.94–1.14. The residual sharp-vs-flat gap (0.05–0.20) is comparable to the unexplained normalisation offset (R_flat sits at 0.88–0.93 where noise demands 1.00).
  • Pair 2→3 shows almost nothing (R ≈ 0.94–0.96 at matched cone angle) while its neighbours show the largest effects — in both samples. A quantity that depends on where you are in the scan but not on which walnut you scanned is not object-dependent detector error.
Confounds 3 and 4 — found during this experiment

Three further mechanisms each produced a convincing false trend before being controlled. Resampling: correcting a −0.3 voxel misregistration on one arm only cuts noise variance ×0.58 in flat regions and blurs at edges. Estimator: medians of absolute differences do not obey the variance algebra when σ varies within a bin. View angles: pairing two even-view sets lets sparse-view streaks cancel while the control's even-vs-odd pairing lets them add — that alone held the flat bins at 0.76.

Verdict: not established, and the replication argues against the hypothesis. The test as designed cannot separate object-dependent detector error from reconstruction geometry, and the effect that survives is reproducible by scan position across two different specimens — the opposite of what object-dependent error predicts. Crucially, no pair of bed positions ever views the same voxel at the same signed cone angle — positions are 15 mm apart and the FOV is 21.84 mm — so R at α = 0 is an upper bound, not a clean measurement.

Open questions

  • Charge summing. Whether this detector performs on-chip charge summing decides whether the diagonal Σ is a real physical finding or evidence the data was pre-corrected. A documentation question, not a compute one, and it gates publishability of the entire null.
  • What the residual actually is. At matched cone angle a small, highly reproducible excess remains (R = 1.07–1.18 on two of three pairs in both samples). It is too consistent to be noise and too position-locked to be the object. Detector-row dependence is the natural next suspect, which connects it to Test 0's module-aligned residual — but that is a hypothesis, not a result.
  • Residual module structure. Test 0's rank-2 module-aligned residual at 1.38× the noise floor is the one real, unexplained effect found. It propagates to rings and bands rather than grain, so it matters more visually than its RMS suggests.
